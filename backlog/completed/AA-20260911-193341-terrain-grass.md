# Add green and snow-covered terrain grass with wind and skier bending

## Outcome

Add regular green grass and snow-white, snow-covered grass to suitable rocky
terrain, forest floors and thinner snow. The user selected **natural alpine
patches, with denser pockets in forests**, and **wind sway plus temporary bending
as the skier passes**. Grass should make these surfaces feel inhabited and varied
while preserving readable terrain and responsive high-speed skiing.

The user explicitly requires **no rock of any kind in the grass assets**. Every
grass asset must be a standalone vegetation asset, containing grass and optional
snow coating on its blades only, independently placeable on different surfaces.

## Agreed decisions and scope

- Natural, irregular alpine clumps with variation in height, orientation and
  density; denser forest pockets and sparser growth on suitable rocky shelves
  and crevices. Preserve substantial bare ground and clear snow. No uniform lawn.
- Include green blades and visibly snow-white coated blades, with mixed or
  partially coated tufts at transitions. White grass must retain blade detail,
  shading and a grounded base rather than appearing as bright flat cutouts.
- Use existing ground conditions to control eligibility, coating and visible
  height. Grass can protrude through shallow snow and becomes buried/suppressed
  in deeper cover. Do not blanket steep cliffs, glaciers or deep powder.
- Wind follows current presentation weather. Nearby grass bends away from the
  skier's passage and recovers shortly afterward; roots remain anchored. This is
  visual interaction only, with no grass collision, drag or changed ski response.
- Scope is grass/low grassy ground cover and consistency with existing mineral
  grass. New shrubs, flowers, trees, seasons, snow accumulation simulation and
  persistent flattened trails are outside this task. No full mineral re-export
  or new tool purchase is needed merely to introduce ground cover.
- Reuse the supplied generator/image where suitable. Preserve the original
  `.blend` and the user's open Blender session; use separate background authoring
  and additive editable sources if improved tufts or textures are needed.
- **Grass-only assets are mandatory.** Do not export or embed any rocks, stones,
  rock fragments, display pedestals, terrain/ground chunks, or rock collision in
  a grass mesh or scene. Reusing the rock generator means extracting only its
  vegetation/image, never exporting the combined rock-and-grass result. Grass
  may be placed over an independently existing rock in the world; that rock must
  remain entirely outside the grass asset and its resource dependencies.
- Keep terrain heights, material/contact authority, physical obstacle placement,
  the 120 Hz solver, input, race results and replay interpretation unchanged.
  Presentation selection uses its own deterministic random stream.

## Implementation approach

1. Establish a reusable tuft asset/material with economical near geometry,
   mipmapped cutout textures, distance reduction and a common green-to-snow
   treatment. Review the existing texture before deciding whether it needs a
   derivative. Confirm actual plant scale, base origin and import settings.
   Integrate shared lighting/cloud shadow and weather wind. Retain an independent
   coating amount so snowy foliage is not implemented as a uniform white tint.
   Verify each exported grass mesh/scene and its dependencies contain vegetation
   only. Do not use a combined mineral GLB or moss/grass mineral scene as the grass
   asset. Any support rock used for authoring or preview must be excluded from
   the grass export; snow detail belongs on blades, not on a supplied ground slab.
2. Add a bounded presentation placement/preparation component, separate from
   physical tree and geology generation. Combine forest suitability, local
   slope, rock/snow coverage, available snow depth, and seeded clustered variation.
   Keep the visible terrain classification aligned with the rendered contact
   mask; use the coarse environment image only as a broad suitability input.
   Read exact terrain support for root seating. For grass on elevated mineral
   surfaces reuse grass-only overlay geometry or independent tuft transforms
   fitted to those surfaces instead of placing another tuft at the underlying
   heightfield. Keep the supporting rock as a separate world asset.
3. Define and document how **snow coverage**, **coating** and **burial depth**
   differ. Raw `snow_depth_at` is not exposed-ground cover. Apply depth only to
   snowy portions, relate burial to the authored blade height, and verify that
   suitable transition areas actually exist on the Standard mountain. Tune plant
   height and distribution to existing surfaces; do not thin the physical mantle
   or paint misleading terrain to manufacture a grass habitat. A test fixture
   alone is insufficient delivery of the requested thin-snow appearance.
4. Build spatial MultiMesh batches with shared resources, conservative bounds
   including wind/bending displacement, and bounded near-camera residency.
   Avoid a Node, body, timer or CPU update per tuft; avoid scanning the entire
   mountain every frame. Use stable cell/seed ordering so streaming and quality
   changes do not reshuffle the landscape. Remove/reuse overlapping old mineral
   grass where necessary to prevent duplicate cards and inconsistent treatment.
5. Feed a bounded visual influence from the skier's swept path into the grass
   shader or nearby interaction state. Cover fast traversal without gaps;
   constrain it by height/proximity so an airborne skier does not flatten ground
   grass far below. Recover smoothly, respect paused presentation, and clear
   influence on retry, teleport, mountain replacement and teardown. Do not use
   camera movement as a skier interaction and do not feed it back to simulation.
6. Extend the existing presentation preparation/cache and cancellation lifecycle
   for any persisted grass data. Version changed scenery payloads, validate
   counts/bounds and include scripts, shaders, meshes and textures in
   [generation source identity](../../scripts/world/generation_sources.gd) and
   exported dependency receipts. Reject stale/corrupt payloads atomically; retain
   no legacy loader solely for prior caches. Do not bump the physical model or
   generator version solely for decoration. Source-based cache invalidation must
   still follow the existing identity rules.
7. Use existing decorative foliage density/distance controls and quality anchors;
   settings changes must take effect coherently for ground and mineral grass.
   Give density zero a real off path, bound shadows/overdraw and fade detail
   without conspicuous popping. Keep render-setting changes out of gameplay data.
   Update the maintained world/graphics docs with placement, asset workflow,
   cache identities, measured costs and remaining opportunities. Follow
   [engine strategy](../../docs/ARCHITECTURE.md#engine-strategy) incrementally.
