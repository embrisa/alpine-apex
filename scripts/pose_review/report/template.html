02 / WHERE TO LOOK

Four comparisons worth pausing on

Identical pixel crops from the recorded full-body cameras. Open an image for the full size. Crops are for posture; inspect the full views for complete pole clearance.

A broader arm silhouette

At this right-turn moment, the measured hand span grows by 9.7 cm. Follow the gloves outward from the shoulders. The elbows and pole angles help make the change visible; the skis are identical.

Check the reach from the side

The side view reveals changes in arm reach and bend that the front view can hide. The lower body looks very close in this pair. The broader arms may read as more active or more stiff; that is a preference to judge in motion.

Sometimes the result really is similar

R6’s source edit passes through the game’s posture and joint fitting. Much of its compression shape gets replaced. Similar final poses are evidence of a small visible payoff here, even though the source animation was successfully imported.

The deeper stance has a different cause

R9 changes physical support beneath the skis and retains R7’s hand pose. The reported deepest-carve boot separation reached 16.94 cm in world height. That is not snow depth, and the trial introduced two new pole/clothing failure frames.

My reading: R7 gives us a visible carving style choice, but these experiments do not establish Cascadeur as the better authoring tool. A pose can be different without being better.

Make the judgement concrete
